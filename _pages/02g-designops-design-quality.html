Design leadership / Strategy

Building a culture of design excellence

Establishing shared quality standards, strengthening collaboration between design and engineering, and making great design a repeatable team practice.

01 · The standard

The Design Quality Framework

Six checks a designer runs on their own work before anyone else sees it. They are written as questions rather than rules, because the point is to make a designer argue with their own screen, not to pass an inspection. The quoted line on each card is the one the original deck leads with.

01

Design intent

“I love my design, but only when…”

  • Does every element on the screen deliver value?
  • Will the user achieve their goal?
  • Is it persona-checked?
  • What could make the design fail?
  • Does it meet UX metrics, if any?

02

Error resilience

“Bad error handling paired with useless error messages will make your visitors leave.”

  • Are all forms and input fields error-handled?
  • What does the error page look like?
  • Can users recover from the error?

03

Design system integrity

“Consistency is key, and the design system is the single source of truth.”

  • Did you reinvent elements that already exist in the design system?
  • Have you picked the right components for the use case?
  • Did you introduce new fonts, icons, colours or shades unnecessarily?

04

Interaction feedback

“When users click and nothing happens, they look for an exit or click again.”

  • Have you considered micro-interactions?
  • Did you use animation while content is loading?
  • Does every meaningful user action provide feedback?

05

Content quality

“Writing copy needs experience too.”

  • Do you speak the same language as your audience?
  • What does the user need to know on this screen?
  • Avoid jargon. Keep content short and easy to understand.

06

Delivery readiness

“A prototype that gives a real experience.”

  • Did you cover all scenarios to test the journeys?
  • Did you replace dummy data with real data?
  • Did you remove unnecessary duplication?

02 · The handoff

Designed to be built right

Make sure your developers have an effortless experience. A design is not finished when it looks right; it is finished when someone else can build it without having to guess what you meant.

DesignThe screen, and every state it can reach.
→
SpecificationStates, interactions and behaviour, written down.
→
EngineeringBuilt against the spec, not against a screenshot.
→
QAChecked against the same document both sides read.
  • Document every error state, empty state and loading state.
  • Specify interactions and animation behaviour.
  • Keep designs aligned with the design system.
  • Make developer handoff clear and actionable.

03 · The practice

From standards to everyday practice

A checklist that stays a document changes nothing. What follows is how the framework showed up in the way the team worked, described as capabilities rather than as results: these are changes in practice, and none of them is a measured outcome.

01

Quality ownership

Designers take responsibility for the complete experience, beyond individual screens.

02

Consistent execution

Shared design system patterns and review standards create consistency across products.

03

Stronger design and engineering partnership

Clear specifications and documented states reduce ambiguity during development.

04

Continuous improvement

Reviews become a way to identify gaps, challenge assumptions and improve the quality of delivery.

Design quality isn't a final checkpoint. It's a shared responsibility built into every stage of the process.
