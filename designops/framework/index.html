DesignOps / 01

A structured process is what keeps discovery honest

It keeps design deliberate and handoff boring, too. This is the sequence I run: research and strategy, concept and solutioning, UX and UI, then review and testing. The activities that belong to each stage are written down, so a team can pick up any one of them without guessing.

Not every activity runs on every project. The framework is the full set; scope decides which parts apply.

Stage 01 · Discover 9 activities

Discover

Research, insights and strategy. The stage that decides what is worth building before anyone draws a screen.

01

Research strategy & planning

Defining the objectives, cohort, sample size, methods and techniques, and a day-wise plan to conduct research activities.

02

User research

Qualitative and quantitative research to understand users and their behaviours, preferences and pain points.

03

Product research

Studying clickstream, existing data and analytics, and industry benchmarks to understand how users behave and interact with the product.

04

Persona development

Built from research findings to represent different user segments, their goals, motivations and behaviours.

05

User journey mapping

Mapping the journey to identify pain points, touchpoints and opportunities for improvement.

06

Empathy mapping

A deeper read on users' thoughts, feelings and motivations at each stage of their journey.

07

Affinity mapping

Organising research findings to synthesise information and identify user patterns and insights.

08

Design problem statement & research insights

Articulating the problems to be solved and the goals to be achieved, including success metrics.

09

UX strategy

Drawing the problems and goals together from stakeholder input, user journey challenges, existing stats and usage analytics, and industry benchmarks against technical feasibility.

Stage 02 · Design 4 activities

Design

Concept ideation and solutioning. Breadth before depth: directions explored and structured before anything is made precise.

01

Solutioning

Whiteboarding, sketching and mind mapping to explore ideas and concepts, uncover insights and identify potential design directions: features, functionality, interaction models, user journeys.

02

User flow & information architecture

Mapping the navigational structure and the sequence of steps users take to accomplish their goals.

03

Define use cases

Writing stories that capture specific user scenarios, tasks and goals.

04

Design prototype

Low-fidelity wireframes of key screens on critical journeys, to visualise navigation, layout structure, features and information hierarchy.

Stage 03 · Detailed design 8 activities

Detailed design

UX and UI through to prototyping, and the documentation that lets engineering build it without a translation layer.

01

Wireframes

Detailing every scenario and journey in the product, covering layout structure, interaction model, information hierarchy and content.

02

Visual design strategy

Exploring visual directions through moodboards, a match against the user persona, benchmarks and technical feasibility.

03

UI design

Applying visual design principles and the design language to produce the mock-ups that set the interface's look and feel.

04

Interactive prototyping

Clickable prototypes with motion and micro-interactions, to sharpen usability and engagement.

05

Responsive & adaptive design

Scaling the design across screen sizes, resolutions and devices.

06

Accessibility considerations

Meeting the baseline accessibility standards and guidelines.

07

UX writing

Clear, concise, user-centric microcopy that guides and engages, and that users can actually understand.

08

Design documentation & handoff

Style guides and design specifications, with assets, to support implementation.

Stage 04 · Validation 6 activities

Validation

Design review and testing. Where the work meets people who did not make it.

01

Strategy & planning

Defining the objectives and goals of the reviews, and identifying the persona, scenarios and tasks to be tested. Creating a screener and onboarding participants.

02

Usability testing

Preparing the questionnaire, the testing guide and the mock-ups.

03

Analysing & synthesising findings

Working through observations, feedback and usability metrics to identify the key findings and insights.

04

Reviewing design artefacts

Expert reviews and heuristic evaluations of wireframes, prototypes and mock-ups.

05

Routine design reviews for QA

Evaluating visual design, layout and aesthetics against established design principles and best practice.

06

Documentation

A usability testing report or presentation, to communicate results to stakeholders.

04 · The intent

Why write it down

The aim is a repeatable, connected process: each stage hands something specific to the next, the activities within it are named rather than assumed, and anyone joining mid-project can see where the work is and what is still open.

Written down, it is something a team can run, argue with and improve, which is more useful than a process that only exists in the head of whoever ran it last.

Arushi Prasad · Design leader

Pune, India
